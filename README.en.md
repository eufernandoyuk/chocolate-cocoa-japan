# 🍫 Chocolate: Value Addition in the Cocoa Chain and the Opportunity of the Japanese Market for Brazil

Academic paper for an **Introduction to Economics** course, including reference research, the final article, and a slide presentation.

## 📌 About the project

This paper investigates Brazil's position in the global cocoa and chocolate value chain — comparing exports of the raw material with exports of the industrialized product — and assesses the potential of the Japanese chocolate market as a destination for Brazilian exports, in light of the reciprocal visa exemption between Brazil and Japan, in effect since September 2023.

**Key findings:**
- Brazil is the world's 7th-largest producer and 7th-largest exporter of cocoa and its derivatives, serving more than 135 countries.
- Even so, Brazil exports more raw/semi-processed cocoa (US$ 228 million) than industrialized chocolate (US$ 142 million) — while the world chocolate market (US$ 33.3 billion) is ~44% larger than the world cocoa market (US$ 23.1 billion).
- Brazil's share of Japan's chocolate imports is only **0.19%** (US$ 1.11 million out of US$ 587 million imported by Japan).
- The reciprocal Brazil-Japan visa exemption (September 30, 2023) lowers transaction costs for business travel and trade prospecting, a favorable — though not sufficient on its own — factor for closer trade ties between the two countries.

## 🔧 Methodology

Foreign-trade data were organized from secondary sources — ApexBrasil and The Atlas of Economic Complexity (Growth Lab, Harvard Kennedy School) — and complemented with research on the diplomatic context of the visa exemption (Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs). The theoretical framework draws on classic international-trade concepts (comparative advantage, value chain, and transaction costs).
